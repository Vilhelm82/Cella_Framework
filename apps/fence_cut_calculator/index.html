    Setup
    How you measure
    Gap: one reading per post, plus the top rail's rake if it has one. String line: two readings per post, for any rails.
    Top rail
    The factory end of each sheet goes against a level rail. A raked top rail gets its rake cut into the top of every sheet.
    Hold a sheet plumb against the top rail with its square top touching the rail at one corner. The gap at the other corner is this number, and that corner is the side the rail rises toward. Bays that differ can override it.

    Why two readings are enough

    1. Straight rails make a straight line of lengths

    Between two posts each rail is straight, so the length a sheet needs changes steadily from one post to the next. A straight line is fixed by two points, so a reading at each post tells you everything. A post shared by two bays gives one reading to both, so a run of N bays needs N + 1 readings. A raked top rail adds one more number, its rise across a sheet. Measuring each sheet edge on site takes two readings per sheet.

    2. The same step from sheet to sheet

    The sheets are identical and evenly spaced, so the length changes by the same amount from one sheet to the next:

    step = (right reading − left reading) ÷ sheets in the bay

    So every sheet in the bay gets the same cut angle, and each sheet's long edge equals the next sheet's short edge. The marks run 1500, 1520, 1540, 1560. You can check the list in your head.

    3. Cut the end that meets the sloping rail

    A square end only sits flush on a level rail. On a rail that slopes, it touches at one corner and gaps at the other by the slope times the sheet width. If you use the rail-to-rail length with the square end on the sloping rail, the far corner jams by that same amount. Put the factory end on the level rail and cut the other end. If the top rail is raked, rake the top of every sheet first. That cut is the same on every sheet, so it's one flush stack, and the lengths down from the raked top are your readings again. A small rake can stay square if its corner gap hides in the channel.

    4. Getting the marks onto the steel, fewest steps first

    1. Chalk line. Lay the bay's sheets face up, lapped as they'll be fitted, factory ends against a straight edge. They now sit exactly as in the fence, so one line from the left reading to the right reading marks every sheet. Two marks, one snap, no sums.
    2. Stack. Nest the sheets and slide each one's factory end out by the step. Every cut line then lands in the same place, so one cut through the stack does the whole bay. Only do this if your saw and blade are rated for the stack.
    3. Sheet by sheet. Mark the left and right numbers from the cut list on each sheet, join them, cut.
